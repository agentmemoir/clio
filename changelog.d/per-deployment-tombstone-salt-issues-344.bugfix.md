Tombstone subject hashes now use a random per-deployment salt recorded once beside the erasure journal, so an unset `CLIO_COMPLIANCE_SALT` no longer falls back to a published constant that let a ledger reader confirm a guessed subject; a journal recorded under a different salt, such as one written before this change under the old compiled-in default, now fails closed instead of silently answering clear: the erasure-denial probe answers the erasure authority as unavailable, an erase refuses to append a denial under a salt the journal was not written under, and `clio backup create` refuses while the journal's recorded fingerprint does not match the live salt rather than recording an archive a restore must reject. Setting `CLIO_COMPLIANCE_SALT` to the salt an existing journal was recorded under keeps that journal authoritative; without it, the denials recorded under the old salt cannot be verified, and the `<journal>.salt` sidecar is now read only as one owner-only regular file, never through a symbolic link and never at a mode that grants group or other access.
